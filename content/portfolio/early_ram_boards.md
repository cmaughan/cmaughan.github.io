These are two RAM expansion designs from one of my earliest hardware jobs, before I started my electronics degree. I was working part-time for a local hardware firm and learning PCB design on real products.

<!--more-->

The blue board is a six-layer RAM board for the Acorn Archimedes. I designed and routed it using a great tool at the time: BoardMaker. The smaller design was causing trouble because its constraints did not sit comfortably with the limits of the manufacturing process (it failed DRC checks). We were only willing to pay so much for the boards, or it would not have been economic. I took it home over the holidays, worked through the layout, and figured it out. The result went on to be quite a successful Archimedes board.

The transparent sheet underneath is the routing artwork for another RAM design, this time for the Amiga. I designed that one with a colleague. I used to really enjoy this challenge; there is something quite meditative about routing prolbems like this. Like solving a complex maze - perhaps that's why I've worked on software Maze generation too.
